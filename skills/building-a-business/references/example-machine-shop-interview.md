# Machine shop interview (v2: with the interview rules and the surfacing block)

Use after an owner has said yes to the conversation. This is not the cold-call opener. One question at a time, plain words. "Yes", "no", "unknown", a number, or a sentence are all fine. Never fill in a missing answer. Never promise savings or recovered revenue.

Pre-filled items below came from a 2026-10-06 role-play, not a real owner. They are tagged **imagined** and go on the to-test list.

## 0. Before I ask (surfacing-unasked-questions block, said in plain words)

Disclosed: I know nothing about your shop yet. I'm assuming you're a small job shop that takes custom quote requests. I haven't seen your numbers.
Three things I'll ask along the way: what decision this should change for you, how you'd know if I'm wrong, and what happens if nothing changes. Skip any you like.

## 1. The questions

1. **In one sentence, what's the main thing you'd like to fix or improve in the shop?**
   *Imagined answer: quoting takes too long.*
2. **Who's waiting on those quotes, and what happens to them while they wait?**
   - Probe: **"How do you know they left because of the wait?"** Record the answer as *declared*, not verified.
3. **A year from now, if that were fixed, what would it look like?**
   - Probe any absolute ("always", "24/7", "never miss one"): *"In the first few minutes after a customer reaches out, what would you want to happen: a reply that says we have it, a booked time, or the price?"*
   - **Read back** the answer in one line and ask "did I get that right?" before recording it.
4. Ask separately:
   - **a.** Who does the quoting today, and how much of their time does it take?
   - **b.** What do they use (paper, spreadsheet, email, software), and do you keep old quotes anywhere?
   - **c.** Roughly how many requests a week, how long does one quote take, and how many do you think you lose? A guess is fine. If unknown, write "measure first" and suggest a two-week tally.
5. **What can't you afford to lose or break while fixing this?** (key customers, quality, your name)
6. **How many hours a week and how much money could you put toward this?**
7. **Who decides, and who would actually use whatever we set up?**
8. **What would make you stop or change direction?**
9. (From the surfacing block) **If you did nothing for a year, what happens?** and **What decision should this conversation change for you?**

## 2. Close

Read the whole thing back in five lines. Ask: "What did I get wrong?" Then say what happens next: a one-page plan for catching and booking requests, with nothing to sign.

## 3. Outputs

`intent.md` (goal, customer, constraints, resources, authority, stop conditions, unknowns) plus the to-test list.

## 4. To-test list (all imagined until a real owner says them)

| Claim | Tag | Question for a real owner |
|---|---|---|
| Slow quotes lose customers who don't come back | imagined | "When a request waits a day, what happens to it?" |
| The owner is the bottleneck, catching calls between jobs | imagined | "When a request comes in while you're on a job, what happens to it?" |
| Booking first, then quoting, would solve it | imagined | "If a customer could book a time first and get the price after, would that help?" |

## 5. Do not

- Do not claim a result. Capacity released is not money saved.
- Do not ask about software accounts until they are interested.
- Do not treat the imagined answers as evidence.
