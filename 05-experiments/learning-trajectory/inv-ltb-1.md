# INV-LTB-1 — Problem-Resistance Terrain Benchmark

## Purpose

Invert the unit of analysis.

LTB measures learner trajectory. INV-LTB measures problem terrain.

The learner is an observational probe; an array of learners and models is the instrument set.

## Terrain dimensions

These are dimensions, not an ordered difficulty scale.

### T1 — Entry cost

Effort and resource distribution required before coherent engagement.

### T2 — Extraction demand

Information that must be extracted before meaningful progress.

Record necessary, optional, and disputed extraction.

### T3 — Generalization demand

Structural transformations required before previously learned strategies cease to be sufficient.

The instance ladder must be independently ordered by manipulated structural demand; raw item number is not a difficulty scale.

### T4 — Exhaustion gradient

Where additional effort stops producing proportional progress.

Record per-learner plateau points and cross-learner variance.

### T5 — Destruction requirement

Prior beliefs or representations that causally obstruct progress.

Candidate destruction features require intervention testing:

retain belief → outcome
remove belief → outcome
restore belief → outcome

## Arms

### DESTROY

Begin with a deliberately incorrect engagement and diagnose the failure.

### CONSTRUCT

Receive only the raw problem and construct an engagement from scratch.

### TERRAIN-INHERIT

Receive a prior terrain map and test whether it predicts useful properties of the problem.

Terrain transfer and capability transfer are separate experiments.

## Divergence

Preserve each independent map, overlap, unique features, and unresolved features.

Divergence is an observation, not automatically evidence of richness, shallowness, or error. Its cause is evaluated separately.

## Stability

Repeat mapping at later epochs.

Measure feature stability, divergence stability, and transfer stability.

## Output

The final artifact is a structured terrain profile rather than a single terrain score.
