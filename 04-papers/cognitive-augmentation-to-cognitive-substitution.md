# From Cognitive Augmentation to Cognitive Substitution

## Human Cognitive Agency in Persistent AI Interaction

**White Paper**
**October 2026**

---

## Abstract

Large language models have rapidly evolved from information-retrieval systems into persistent conversational systems capable of reasoning, synthesis, coding, planning, explanation, persuasion, and collaborative problem solving.

This creates a new category of human–machine interaction.

The central question is no longer simply whether artificial intelligence improves human task performance. It is whether repeated interaction with increasingly capable conversational systems changes **how humans formulate problems, generate hypotheses, evaluate evidence, construct beliefs, and determine what constitutes a reasonable conclusion**.

This paper introduces a distinction between **cognitive augmentation** and **cognitive substitution**.

Cognitive augmentation occurs when AI expands a person's cognitive capability while preserving meaningful human control over problem formulation, exploration, evaluation, and judgment.

Cognitive substitution occurs when AI increasingly performs those higher-order functions on the user's behalf, particularly when the user begins adopting the system's framing, conceptual vocabulary, evaluation criteria, or conclusions as the starting point for subsequent independent thought.

The paper identifies **framing substitution** as a potentially important mechanism. A user presents an unfinished thought; the AI repeatedly converts that thought into a polished, conventional, qualified, or “defensible” formulation. Over repeated interactions, those formulations can become the user's subsequent cognitive starting points.

This phenomenon differs from ordinary cognitive offloading because the system may not merely perform a task. It may participate in determining **what the task is, how the problem should be understood, and which forms of reasoning are considered legitimate**.

Recent research provides evidence for several components of this model. Work on generative-AI cognitive offloading distinguishes between dependent offloading, where core thinking is delegated, and autonomous offloading, where AI functions as a scaffold while cognitive agency remains with the user. Research on belief offloading similarly identifies the possibility that users delegate portions of belief formation and maintenance to AI systems. Other recent work reports that AI-assisted performance can improve while mental effort decreases, without necessarily producing corresponding improvements in independent reasoning or time efficiency.

The central proposition of this paper is therefore:

> **The relevant unit of evaluation for persistent conversational AI is not only model capability, but the trajectory of the human who repeatedly interacts with that capability.**

The paper proposes a longitudinal research framework—the **Human Cognitive Agency Benchmark (HCAB)**—for measuring whether AI interaction increases, preserves, or displaces independent human cognitive capability.

# 1. Introduction

Artificial intelligence is increasingly becoming conversational infrastructure.

A user can now maintain an ongoing interaction with a model across research, programming, planning, education, writing, analysis, and personal decision-making.

The interaction is therefore no longer adequately described as:

> Human asks question → machine returns answer.

Instead:

> Human produces thought → machine interprets thought → machine produces framing → human responds to framing → machine responds to modified human thought.

This creates a recursive cognitive environment.

The model's output becomes part of the context from which the human produces the next thought.

Over time, the system can therefore influence more than the answer to an individual question.

It can influence:

* problem formulation,
* vocabulary,
* conceptual categories,
* hypotheses,
* standards of evidence,
* interpretation,
* confidence,
* evaluation,
* and subsequent questions.

This possibility does not imply that AI is inherently harmful.

The same recursive mechanism can produce extraordinary cognitive augmentation.

The central problem is therefore not:

> **AI or no AI?**

It is:

> **What kind of cognitive relationship is being created between the human and the model?**

---

# 2. Cognitive Augmentation vs. Cognitive Substitution

## 2.1 Cognitive augmentation

Cognitive augmentation occurs when AI increases a person's ability to:

* explore possibilities,
* generate alternatives,
* construct systems,
* test hypotheses,
* identify errors,
* retrieve information,
* simulate outcomes,
* and perform complex work,

while the person retains meaningful authority over:

* direction,
* interpretation,
* evaluation,
* and final judgment.

The model expands the human's cognitive search space.

---

## 2.2 Cognitive substitution

Cognitive substitution occurs when AI increasingly performs cognitive operations that the human previously performed independently.

These operations may include:

* determining what the problem is,
* deciding which interpretation is reasonable,
* generating the relevant hypotheses,
* determining which evidence matters,
* deciding which alternatives deserve consideration,
* constructing the argument,
* and producing the conclusion.

The key distinction is therefore **not workload**.

A human can delegate enormous amounts of mechanical work without losing cognitive agency.

The important question is:

> **Which cognitive operations have been delegated?**

---

# 3. Cognitive Offloading Is Not the Whole Problem

Cognitive offloading is not new.

Humans routinely use external systems to reduce cognitive demands.

Examples include:

* calculators,
* notebooks,
* maps,
* calendars,
* search engines,
* reference books,
* software,
* and automated tools.

Offloading can increase human capability.

The problem becomes more complex with generative AI because the system can perform **open-ended cognitive operations** rather than merely bounded calculations.

A calculator performs arithmetic.

A conversational model can decide how to interpret the question.

Recent research explicitly distinguishes **dependent cognitive offloading** from **autonomous cognitive offloading**. Dependent offloading delegates core thinking to AI, while autonomous offloading uses AI as a scaffold while preserving the user's cognitive agency.

This distinction is fundamental.

The question is not:

> “Did the person use AI?”

It is:

> **“How was cognition allocated during the interaction?”**

---

# 4. Belief Offloading

A further development occurs when AI becomes involved in belief formation.

Recent work introduces the concept of **belief offloading**, describing situations in which people delegate parts of the processes through which beliefs are formed and maintained to AI systems.

This creates a distinction between:

### Information offloading

> “Tell me what happened.”

and:

### Belief offloading

> “Tell me what this means, whether I should believe it, and how I should interpret it.”

The second operation is substantially more consequential.

If repeated interaction causes the user to rely on the model not merely for information but for **epistemic interpretation**, the model begins occupying a position within the user's cognitive authority structure.

---

# 5. The Cognitive Authority Structure

This paper defines a **cognitive authority structure** as the set of processes through which an individual determines:

* what is true,
* what is plausible,
* what is important,
* what evidence is sufficient,
* what interpretations are reasonable,
* and what conclusions should be accepted.

Traditional information systems can influence this structure.

Conversational AI can participate in it directly.

This creates a potential progression:

### Level 0 — Instrument

AI performs bounded mechanical operations.

**Human:** direction and judgment
**AI:** execution

---

### Level 1 — Assistant

AI provides information, suggestions, and alternatives.

**Human:** evaluates outputs
**AI:** proposes

---

### Level 2 — Collaborator

Human and AI recursively generate, test, and refine ideas.

**Human:** retains meaningful judgment
**AI:** expands cognitive search

---

### Level 3 — Cognitive Authority

AI increasingly determines:

* the framing,
* the relevant evidence,
* the interpretation,
* the alternatives,
* and the acceptable conclusion.

**Human:** increasingly validates or adopts

**AI:** increasingly governs cognitive trajectory

The transition from Level 2 to Level 3 is the primary concern of this paper.

---

# 6. Framing Substitution

This paper proposes **framing substitution** as a specific mechanism through which cognitive substitution can occur.

### Definition

> **Framing substitution is the repeated replacement of a user's original or unfinished problem framing with an AI-generated framing that subsequently becomes the basis for further reasoning.**

Consider:

**Human:**

> “Something strange is happening here.”

**AI:**

> “A more defensible interpretation is…”

The immediate response may appear helpful.

But repeated thousands of times, the interaction can produce:

**Observation → AI framing → human adoption → future observation interpreted through AI framing.**

The model has not merely answered.

It has modified the conceptual lens through which the next question is generated.

---

# 7. The Defensibility Trap

A particularly important version of framing substitution occurs when AI systems systematically optimize human statements for defensibility.

This behavior has legitimate purposes.

It can:

* reduce overclaiming,
* distinguish fact from inference,
* improve precision,
* reduce misunderstandings,
* and prevent unsupported conclusions.

But continual optimization for defensibility may produce a different problem:

## Premature convergence.

Exploration often requires temporarily allowing ideas to remain:

* incomplete,
* speculative,
* contradictory,
* emotionally expressed,
* unusual,
* poorly articulated,
* or wrong.

These states are not necessarily failures.

They can be intermediate states in discovery.

If every unfinished idea is immediately transformed into the model's preferred polished formulation, the model may reduce the exploratory search space **before the human has finished searching it**.

The result can be:

> **thought → qualification → convergence**

instead of:

> **thought → expansion → alternatives → contradiction → testing → convergence**

This distinction is central.

---

# 8. Why Conversational AI Is Different

Persistent conversational AI has several properties that distinguish it from many previous cognitive tools.

## 8.1 Linguistic fluency

The system can express its interpretation more coherently than the user's unfinished thought.

## 8.2 Personalization

The system can adapt responses to the user's previous interaction.

## 8.3 Availability

The system can be consulted continuously.

## 8.4 Recursion

Each response becomes context for subsequent responses.

## 8.5 Breadth

The same system can participate in reasoning across many domains.

## 8.6 Apparent authority

Fluent explanations can create an impression of epistemic competence even when the underlying answer is wrong.

Together, these properties create a persistent cognitive environment rather than a conventional tool interaction.

---

# 9. The Longitudinal Problem

Most AI evaluation remains heavily focused on **single-task performance**.

Typical questions include:

* Did the model answer correctly?
* Did the model solve the problem?
* Did productivity increase?
* Did accuracy increase?
* Did task completion improve?

These are important.

They are insufficient.

A person can perform better with AI while simultaneously becoming less capable without it.

Recent research illustrates this tension. A 2026 study of young adults found that AI-assisted participants achieved higher accuracy and lower reported mental effort on a logical-fallacy task, while qualitative analysis showed a mixture of strategic engagement and complete delegation.

A large preregistered study of 1,237 participants also reported a “speedup illusion”: participants expected AI assistance to save more time than it actually did, while subjective effort decreased even when completion times did not.

These findings suggest that **immediate subjective benefit cannot by itself establish long-term cognitive benefit**.

The evaluation horizon must therefore extend beyond the interaction.

---

# 10. Human Cognitive Trajectory

This paper proposes a new evaluation object:

> **Human Cognitive Trajectory (HCT)**

HCT describes how a person's independent cognitive capabilities change across repeated AI interactions.

Relevant dimensions include:

* problem formulation,
* hypothesis generation,
* conceptual diversity,
* independent evaluation,
* disagreement,
* memory,
* confidence calibration,
* linguistic framing,
* epistemic agency,
* and recovery after AI removal.

This changes the fundamental research question.

Instead of:

> “Did AI improve performance?”

we ask:

> **“What happened to the human's independent capability after repeated AI assistance?”**

---

# 11. Human Cognitive Agency Benchmark

We propose the **Human Cognitive Agency Benchmark (HCAB)**.

HCAB is a longitudinal experimental framework designed to distinguish cognitive augmentation from cognitive substitution.

## Condition A — Human Only

Participants complete tasks without AI.

This establishes baseline independent performance.

---

## Condition B — AI Answering

AI provides answers or information but does not actively restructure the user's reasoning process.

This measures basic assistance.

---

## Condition C — AI Collaborative

AI generates:

* alternatives,
* counterexamples,
* questions,
* competing hypotheses,
* tests,
* and critiques.

The AI is explicitly designed to expand the search space.

---

## Condition D — AI Directive

AI provides:

* preferred interpretations,
* recommended conclusions,
* problem framing,
* and explicit direction.

This represents a high-substitution interaction pattern.

---

## Condition E — AI Defensibility

AI systematically transforms user-generated thoughts into more qualified, polished, conventional, or “defensible” formulations.

This isolates the framing-substitution mechanism.

---

# 12. Measurement Framework

HCAB should measure at least ten dimensions.

## 12.1 Framing Independence

Can participants formulate the problem independently after repeated AI interaction?

---

## 12.2 Hypothesis Diversity

How many genuinely distinct explanations do participants generate?

---

## 12.3 Model-Frame Adoption

How frequently do participants subsequently reproduce concepts introduced by the AI?

---

## 12.4 Independent Evaluation

Can participants identify model errors without AI assistance?

---

## 12.5 Disagreement Capacity

Can participants maintain a justified disagreement with a confident model?

---

## 12.6 Cognitive Recovery

What proportion of independent capability remains after AI access is removed?

---

## 12.7 Belief Persistence

Do AI-introduced beliefs or interpretations persist after the interaction?

---

## 12.8 Linguistic Convergence

Does participant language increasingly converge toward recurring model framings?

---

## 12.9 Search-Space Compression

Does repeated interaction reduce the diversity of independently generated hypotheses?

---

## 12.10 Agency Attribution

Can participants correctly identify whether an idea originated from:

* themselves,
* the AI,
* or the interaction between them?

---

# 13. A Critical New Metric: Cognitive Recovery

One of the most important measures proposed by HCAB is **cognitive recovery**.

The experiment is simple.

### Phase 1

Measure independent capability.

### Phase 2

Expose participants to repeated AI interaction.

### Phase 3

Remove AI.

### Phase 4

Repeat equivalent independent tasks.

The critical comparison becomes:

> **Baseline independent capability → post-interaction independent capability**

This prevents AI-assisted performance from being mistaken for human capability.

A system may increase performance while available and decrease independent capability when unavailable.

That is not necessarily augmentation.

It may be substitution.

---

# 14. Search-Space Compression

Another important metric is **search-space compression**.

Suppose a person encounters a difficult problem.

Before AI interaction, they independently generate:

> A, B, C, D, E, F

After prolonged interaction with a model, they generate:

> A, B

because the model's recurring framing has made C–F less cognitively salient.

The person may become more efficient.

They may also have become less exploratory.

Therefore:

> **Efficiency ≠ cognitive expansion.**

A good augmentation system should ideally increase the quality of reasoning without unnecessarily collapsing the diversity of plausible hypotheses.

---

# 15. Model-Frame Adoption

A longitudinal experiment should also examine whether users increasingly adopt model-generated concepts.

For example:

### Session 1

Model introduces concept X.

### Session 5

User independently uses X.

### Session 20

User automatically frames new problems through X.

This does not establish harm.

The adopted concept may be useful.

The measurement question is instead:

> **How much of the user's conceptual vocabulary increasingly originates from the AI?**

This allows researchers to distinguish:

**learning**

from

**unexamined cognitive convergence.**

---

# 16. Augmentation vs. Substitution Matrix

| Dimension              | Augmentation      | Substitution            |
| ---------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------- |
| Problem formulation    | Human-led         | AI-led                  |
| Hypothesis generation  | Expanded          | Delegated               |
| Alternatives           | Increased         | Narrowed                |
| Evaluation             | Human + AI        | AI-dominated            |
| Disagreement           | Preserved         | Reduced                 |
| AI output              | Scaffold          | Authority               |
| Human agency           | Preserved         | Transferred             |
| Independent capability | Stable/increasing | Potentially declining   |
| Cognitive recovery     | High              | Low                     |
| Search space           | Expanded          | Compressed              |
| Belief formation       | Human-directed    | AI-influenced/delegated |

This matrix is not intended to classify all AI use as one category.

The same person can move between categories across tasks.

# 17. Design Principle: Human Capability Optimization

AI systems are commonly optimized around:

> **Answer quality.**

This paper proposes a complementary objective:

> **Human capability preservation and expansion.**

The best response is therefore not always the most complete answer.

Sometimes the optimal response is:

* a counterexample,
* a competing hypothesis,
* a question,
* an experiment,
* a disagreement,
* a partial scaffold,
* or a deliberately unresolved problem.

The objective is to maximize the user's **cognitive capability**, not merely minimize the user's cognitive effort.

---

# 18. Cognitive Friction

This leads to a counterintuitive design principle:

> **Some cognitive friction is valuable.**

If the model removes every difficulty, it may also remove the cognitive activity that produces learning and independent capability.

Useful friction can include:

* asking the user to predict before revealing an answer,
* requiring independent hypothesis generation,
* presenting conflicting explanations,
* withholding premature convergence,
* requiring evidence evaluation,
* and periodically operating without AI.

The goal is not artificial difficulty.

It is preservation of meaningful cognitive participation.

Recent design research similarly argues for preserving cognitive friction, using LLMs as provisional thinking partners, embedding evaluation, and alternating AI-mediated and AI-free phases.

---

# 19. The Human Should Remain the Source of Direction

The central design invariant is:

> **The human should remain the source of cognitive direction even when the machine becomes the source of enormous cognitive capacity.**

This does not require humans to perform every cognitive operation manually.

A person can delegate:

* coding,
* summarization,
* calculations,
* document generation,
* data transformation,
* simulation,
* and repetitive analysis.

The critical boundary concerns **direction and judgment**.

The model can execute enormous amounts of work.

The human should retain meaningful authority over:

> **What are we trying to understand?**

> **What possibilities should we consider?**

> **What counts as evidence?**

> **What should we test?**

> **What do we ultimately believe?**

---

# 20. AIL and the Anti-Substitution Principle

A useful reasoning architecture for preserving agency is to deliberately separate:

**axiom → anomaly → alternative explanation → hypothesis → test → result**

rather than allowing the model to immediately converge on a single explanation.

This structure forces the cognitive process to remain exploratory.

It also creates a useful human–AI division of labor:

### Human

* supplies observations,
* identifies anomalies,
* challenges assumptions,
* chooses questions,
* evaluates results.

### AI

* generates alternatives,
* explores combinations,
* searches hypothesis space,
* constructs tests,
* implements solutions,
* attacks conclusions.

The AI therefore becomes a **mixture-of-possibilities engine** rather than a single-answer authority.

---

# 21. The Risk Is Not Intelligence

The concern described in this paper should not be interpreted as an argument against highly capable AI.

In fact, the opposite may be true.

The more capable the model becomes, the more important the distinction becomes.

A weak model cannot easily replace sophisticated human reasoning.

A highly capable model potentially can.

Therefore:

> **The better the model becomes, the more important human cognitive agency becomes.**

The objective is not to keep AI weak enough that humans remain necessary.

The objective is to build systems in which **human agency scales alongside machine capability**.

# 22. A Research Agenda

The following research questions should be investigated longitudinally.

### RQ1

Does repeated AI interaction change independent problem formulation?

### RQ2

Does repeated model framing increase linguistic convergence?

### RQ3

Does AI assistance increase or decrease hypothesis diversity?

### RQ4

Does repeated AI use alter willingness to disagree with the model?

### RQ5

Does AI-mediated performance predict independent post-interaction capability?

### RQ6

Which interaction patterns produce augmentation?

### RQ7

Which interaction patterns produce substitution?

### RQ8

Can cognitive recovery be measured reliably?

### RQ9

Does explicit metacognitive monitoring prevent cognitive substitution?

### RQ10

Can AI systems be optimized specifically for **human capability expansion rather than answer maximization**?

# 23. Falsifiability

The thesis of this paper should be falsifiable.

The cognitive-substitution hypothesis would be weakened if longitudinal experiments consistently showed that:

1. repeated AI interaction does not alter independent framing;
2. model-generated framing is not preferentially adopted;
3. hypothesis diversity remains stable or increases;
4. disagreement capacity remains stable or increases;
5. cognitive recovery remains high;
6. independent performance improves after AI exposure;
7. belief formation remains substantially independent of model framing.

Conversely, evidence for substitution would increase if repeated AI interaction reliably produces:

1. increased model-frame adoption;
2. reduced independent hypothesis diversity;
3. reduced disagreement capacity;
4. reduced cognitive recovery;
5. increased reliance on model-generated evaluation criteria;
6. persistent adoption of AI-generated beliefs;
7. measurable convergence in conceptual framing.

The objective is not to prove that AI is harmful.

It is to determine **under what interaction conditions augmentation becomes substitution.**

---

# 24. Ethical Implications

AI systems increasingly participate in education, professional judgment, research, personal decision-making, and creative work.

If persistent AI interaction can influence cognitive trajectories, then system design has an obligation to consider more than immediate user satisfaction.

A system that always produces the easiest answer may be highly satisfying.

It may also unintentionally reduce independent cognitive participation.

Ethical design should therefore consider:

* cognitive agency,
* transparency of framing,
* reversibility,
* independent evaluation,
* disagreement,
* cognitive recovery,
* and long-term capability.

The appropriate objective is not paternalistic restriction.

It is **informed cognitive autonomy**.

---

# 25. Conclusion

The defining question of advanced conversational AI may not be:

> **“How intelligent is the machine?”**

It may be:

> **“What happens to the human who thinks with the machine every day?”**

A conversational model can become more than an instrument.

It can become a collaborator.

A collaborator can become a cognitive scaffold.

And a scaffold can become a cognitive authority.

That transition may occur gradually, through ordinary interactions rather than explicit persuasion.

One framing.

One interpretation.

One completed argument.

One accepted recommendation.

One avoided disagreement.

One outsourced decision.

Repeated thousands of times.

The resulting influence may not be visible at the level of an individual conversation.

It may become visible only in the **trajectory of the human over time**.

This paper therefore proposes a shift in AI evaluation.

Do not measure only:

> **What can the model do?**

Also measure:

> **What does repeated interaction with the model do to the human's ability to think independently?**

The objective should not be to minimize human cognitive effort.

Nor should it be to force humans to work without machines.

The objective is:

> **Build AI that makes humans more capable without quietly becoming the authority that determines how they think.**

The strongest human–AI system is not the one in which the human thinks the least.

It is the one in which the human can **think farther because the machine is there.**
