# BlackSwanLabz Company Intelligence & Operational Opportunity Research Manual

### Manufacturing Edition — Fox Valley Pilot

**Version:** 1.0
**Operating principle:** *Find it. Verify it. Measure it. Then automate it.*

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This manual defines the standard operating procedure BlackSwanLabz uses to research a company, identify operational friction, test competing explanations, and determine whether an opportunity should be:

1. **Left alone**
2. **Improved manually/process-wise**
3. **Automated**

BlackSwanLabz does **not** begin with automation.

It begins with evidence.

> **Never confuse finding an opportunity with proving an opportunity.**
>
> **Never confuse proving an opportunity with delivering measurable improvement.**

# 5. RESEARCH PRINCIPLE

The researcher must distinguish five things:

### FACT

Directly supported by evidence.

### OBSERVATION

Something publicly observable but requiring interpretation.

### INFERENCE

A reasonable conclusion derived from multiple observations.

### HYPOTHESIS

A possibility requiring validation.

### UNKNOWN

Insufficient evidence.

Never present an inference as a fact.

# 7. THE 12 RESEARCH LANES

Every target company receives the same basic research pass.

## Lane 1 — Customer

Research:

* Who buys?
* Why do they buy?
* What do customers complain about?
* What do customers praise?
* Where does the customer journey slow down?
* What questions repeat?

Sources:

* Reviews
* Website
* testimonials
* public forums
* industry publications
* social channels

---

## Lane 2 — Employee

Research:

* What positions are repeatedly hired?
* What skills are emphasized?
* What responsibilities repeat?
* What does the company say employees will do?
* Are there signals of turnover or staffing pressure?

Hiring is treated as a **signal**, not proof of a problem.

## Lane 3 — Leadership

Research leadership through:

* Company website
* LinkedIn

* conference appearances
* industry associations

* background

# 18. FIRSTHAND INFORMATION

Employees, former employees, customers, partners, and other firsthand sources can provide valuable hypotheses.

However:

**Firsthand knowledge is not automatically proof.**

Record it as:

```text
SOURCE TYPE: Firsthand
CLAIM: [statement]
CONFIDENCE: Preliminary
STATUS: Requires validation
```

Never disclose confidential information.

Never claim inside knowledge publicly.

Never use proprietary information without authorization.

# 22. MOIE ANALYSIS

Every major hypothesis goes through adversarial analysis.

### Agent 1 — Inversion Critic

Ask:

> What if our interpretation is wrong?

### Agent 2 — Positive Deviant Scout

Ask:

> Where does the company already perform exceptionally well?

### Agent 3 — Mechanism Synthesizer

Ask:

> What mechanism could actually explain the observed pattern?

### Agent 4 — Red Team

Ask:

> What evidence would disprove this opportunity?

### Agent 5 — Prediction Generator

Ask:

> If our hypothesis is correct, what should we observe?

# 25. DECISION ENGINE

Every opportunity must produce one of three recommendations.

## DO NOTHING

Use when:

* evidence is weak
* economics are poor
* risk is excessive
* intervention isn't justified

Explain:

* why
* what would change the recommendation
* when to revisit

---

## IMPROVE

Use when:

* process is valuable
* automation isn't justified
* process discipline is the bottleneck

Examples:

* standard operating procedure
* training
* communication protocol
* ownership clarification
* workflow redesign

---

## AUTOMATE

Use when:

* process is stable
* repetition is high
* rules are understandable
* value is measurable
* integration is feasible
* risk is controlled
* rollback is possible

# 27. VALUE REALIZATION

Separate:

### Capacity Released

Time no longer required for the old process.

from:

### Economic Value

Demonstrated business consequence.

Economic value may come from:

* reduced overtime
* increased capacity
* faster customer response
* reduced rework
* fewer errors
* revenue generation
* reduced administrative burden

Never automatically convert hours into "money saved."

# 45. THE BLACKSWAN RULE

The researcher is not paid to prove that automation is necessary.

The researcher is paid to discover the truth.

Therefore:

```text
If automation is wrong:
     SAY SO.

If process improvement is better:
     SAY SO.

If doing nothing is better:
     SAY SO.

If automation is justified:
     PROVE IT.

If it is deployed:
     MEASURE IT.

If it stops working:
     FIX IT OR RETIRE IT.
```
