# Black Swan Labs Research Group
## Universal People-Centered Research Operating System

> **Intelligence is becoming abundant. Verification isn't.**
>
> Black Swan Labs Research Group is a people-centered research organization built around Love, Safety, Abundance, Growth, Transparency, Never Harm, and the Golden Rule, using governed state, question engineering, reusable skills, harnesses, evidence, and continuous feedback to turn owner intent into research programs and verified capabilities.

## 1. What This Group Is

Black Swan Labs Research Group (BSLRG) is not organized around a fixed list of scientific subjects.

It is organized around a universal research operating architecture.

The group can investigate:

- artificial intelligence
- computer science
- mathematics
- physics
- engineering
- business
- economics
- law
- operations
- biology
- chemistry
- social science
- other domains selected by the research owner

The domain-specific content changes.

The operating system does not.

### Core proposition

> **The group provides the skeleton, methods, skills, harnesses, governance, and research infrastructure; the research owner supplies the question, objective, constraints, and final judgment.**

The research program is instantiated from intent rather than from a prewritten master plan.

# 2. FOUNDING PRINCIPLES

## Love

People are the reason the institution exists.

Research should create capability without treating researchers, operators, collaborators, or customers as disposable inputs.

## Safety

No research result is worth bypassing appropriate safety, authorization, provenance, privacy, or verification controls.

## Abundance

Knowledge, tools, compute, reusable skills, and opportunities should compound.

A successful investigation should leave the group with more capability than it started with.

## Growth

A result is valuable when it teaches the organization something that improves its future work.

Failure is retained as evidence when it produces a useful correction.

## Transparency

The group distinguishes:

- observation
- source
- model assertion
- interpretation
- hypothesis
- experiment
- verified result
- human decision

## Never Harm

Do not intentionally design research or operational practices to exploit or harm people.

## Always Treat Others the Way You Would Love to Be Treated

This applies to:

- employees
- contractors
- collaborators
- research subjects
- customers
- reviewers
- competitors
- users

# 4. THE GROUP'S TWO SYSTEMS

BSLRG contains two coupled systems.

## A. Research Construction System

Turns a research owner's intent into a configured research program.

**Intent -> Questions -> Evidence -> Program Design -> Simulation -> Approval -> Execution**

## B. Research Operating System

Runs and improves the research program.

**Observation -> Question -> Hypothesis -> Experiment -> Evidence -> Verification -> Learning -> Capability -> Next Question**

The first system builds the second.

The second improves the first.

# 5. OWNER INTENT COMPILER

The research owner begins with ordinary language.

Example:

> "I want to know whether this idea actually works."

The system does not demand a formal research protocol immediately.

It asks adaptive questions.

### Intent questions

- What do you want to know?
- Why does it matter?
- What outcome would be useful?
- What would change your mind?
- What should never be sacrificed?
- What constraints matter?
- Who is affected?
- What decisions depend on the answer?
- How much uncertainty is acceptable?
- What authority should the research team have?
- What requires your approval?

The owner can answer using:

- yes/no
- choices
- ranking
- scales
- numbers
- ranges
- short explanations
- longer explanations
- voice input

The question engine chooses the lowest-burden format that can recover the necessary intent.

---

# 6. ADAPTIVE QUESTION ENGINEERING

The engine maintains an Intent State:

- confirmed
- inferred
- provisional
- unknown
- conflicted
- superseded

It asks the next highest-value unresolved question.

Priority considers:

- uncertainty
- decision impact
- dependency
- risk
- answer cost

The priority model is a heuristic.

It must be validated against actual research outcomes rather than treated as a law.

# 7. LINGUISTIC INTENT EXTRACTION

The system analyzes language for hidden structure.

It looks for:

- undefined terms
- absolute words
- hidden obligations
- hidden authority
- temporal ambiguity
- scope ambiguity
- category labels
- unstated actors
- passive constructions
- causal assumptions
- value-laden terms
- implied constraints

Example:

> "Find the truth."

The system asks:

> Truth about what referent?

> In what scope?

> According to which evidence?

> Over what time period?

> What level of verification is required?

The goal is not to burden the owner.

It is to prevent vague intent from silently becoming concrete policy.

# 10. NO MASTER-PLAN DEPENDENCY

The group deliberately avoids requiring a perfect plan before beginning.

Local plans are allowed.

A comprehensive, long-range master plan is not required.

The governing question is:

> **What is the next justified research action?**

The system acts, observes, and updates.

This makes wrong turns cheaper to discover.

# 12. RESEARCH TRIAGE

Classify each proposal:

### EXPLORATORY

Weak prior knowledge; broad discovery.

### EVALUATIVE

Known object; question is whether it works.

### VERIFICATION

Claim exists; determine whether it is supported.

### REPRODUCTION

Existing result; attempt independent reproduction.

### CONSTRUCTION

Build a new system/procedure/capability.

### FORENSIC

Investigate an existing claim, failure, or discrepancy.

### TRANSFER

Test whether a capability works in a new domain.

# 13. AIL RESEARCH PASS

For material assumptions:

**APPARENT AXIOM -> INVERT -> RE-DERIVE -> COMPARE -> TEST -> SYNTHESIZE**

Ask:

- What is being assumed?
- What happens if the assumption is false?
- What competing framework exists?
- What evidence would discriminate?
- Where should the inversion fail?

The group does not treat inversion as proof.

It is a mechanism for finding testable alternatives.

# 15. ANOMALY GATE

Every surprising result is classified before being promoted to discovery.

Possible states:

- factual error
- definition mismatch
- scope mismatch
- measurement mismatch
- temporal difference
- boundary condition
- model difference
- framework difference
- translation error
- source error
- genuine unresolved anomaly

An anomaly that disappears after correcting scope or measurement is not silently promoted into a scientific discovery.

---

# 16. EVIDENCE GATE

The group distinguishes:

**model assertion**

-> **model agreement**

-> **retrieved claim**

-> **primary source**

-> **computational verification**

-> **formal verification**

-> **independent human verification**

-> **replication**

The required level depends on consequence.

Consensus is not automatically independence.

# 22. FALSIFICATION

Every meaningful project must state what would count as failure.

Possible falsifiers:

- no measurable improvement
- unsupported claim

- transfer failure
- adversarial failure
- quality degradation
- governance failure
- intent drift
- excessive cost
- unsafe behavior

> **We tested it and it did not work.**

# 23. ADVERSARIAL RESEARCH

Before a major conclusion, attack it.

Ask:

- What if the measurement is wrong?
- What if the source is stale?
- What if the result is a scope artifact?
- What if multiple models inherited the same error?
- What competing mechanism explains the result?
- What result would falsify this?
- Did the experiment actually discriminate between hypotheses?
- Did we test the intended property?
- Can an independent evaluator reproduce the result?
- Does the conclusion survive a new domain?
