# BlackSwanLabz — Research Service

**Purpose:** Define the exact research-based offer, workflow, pitch, delivery mechanics, and CX principles for the BlackSwanLabz company-research product.

**Related:** BlackSwanLabz-Offering-Guidance (how research and automation connect) · BlackSwanLabz-Funnel (funnel design) · BlackSwanLabz-Deal-Pipeline (prospects)

---

## The offer

### Free — Research Snapshot

A company-specific research package built entirely from public signals (job postings, filings, press, reviews, operational footprints), following the protocol: **signals → convergence → inversion → competing hypotheses → red team → falsifiable prediction**. The snapshot identifies one or more operational patterns worth looking at.

This is a lead-gen product. It is a real deliverable that stands on its own — not a teaser for automation.

### $500–$1,500 (TBD) — Deep Intelligence Report

The full protocol research, for clients who want the intelligence without committing to automation.

**Explore only.** Whether this tier exists is a decision for after the free tier has proven it opens doors and a client expresses willingness to pay for the full analysis.

---

### What the client gets

A single markdown document, delivered as PDF and/or Word in their preferred channel, with a one-page executive summary up front.

The document includes:

1. Target qualification table (6 dimensions × 10/10 max)
2. Signal map (3–6 observed signals with source citations)
3. Signal convergence logic
4. AIL inversion of the core operational assumption
5. Competing hypotheses
6. Red-team assessment
7. Falsifiable prediction

The one-page summary is written in plain English. A reader who stops after page one still gets the point.

---

### What the client does NOT get

- Automation build, deploy, or maintenance
- A care tier or monthly reports
- A guarantee of any outcome
- Financial advice or private data analysis
- Any commitment to a future engagement

If the research surfaces a workflow worth automating, automation is a separate product — sold on its own terms, not bundled. That's documented in BlackSwanLabz-Offering-Guidance.

## Protocol guardrails

- **Public data only.** No NDA, no financials, no internal documents.
- **Protocol is fixed.** All research follows the same 6-step structure regardless of company size.
- **Validation target:** large, sophisticated public manufacturers first — not 100–500-person SMBs. Method must hold at scale before it becomes a packaged deliverable.
- **Sources preferred:** SEC filings, job postings, Glassdoor, Yelp, press releases, public website content, analyst commentary.

---

## CX principles (non-negotiable)

The research is only valuable if the prospect actually reads and uses it. Design every step for **comfort, clarity, and zero technical friction** — especially for owners who are new to this kind of analysis or less technical.

- **Plain language first.** No protocol jargon in the deliverable. Executive summary in plain English; supporting detail follows.
- **Familiar formats only.** Offer PDF and/or Word by default. Markdown/source files are Wilson's working format, not the client-facing deliverable.
- **Channel is their choice.** Ask once: "Would you prefer this as a Gmail attachment, a Google Drive link, or a Dropbox link?" Then use exactly that channel.
- **Cover note always.** Every delivery includes a short, plain-English cover letter explaining what the report is, how to read it, and that no action is required before the follow-up conversation.
- **Warm intro before cold send.** Wilson's first message is short, personal, and references one real signal from the research. The report comes after they express interest or agree to receive it — not as an unsolicited 20-page surprise attachment.
- **No pressure follow-up.** Soft cadence only: no reply in 5 days = one short follow-up. No reply in 10 days = archive for the quarter. No exceptions.
- **Phone/SMS option.** For owners who prefer voice, offer a 10-minute walkthrough call instead of (or in addition to) the written report.
- **Beginner-friendly report structure:** 1-page summary → key findings → competitor snapshot → "what you might do next" → supporting detail. They can stop reading after page one and still get the point.

## Full pitch script (complete conversation flow)

Use this as the full customer-facing script from first touch through follow-up. It is plain language, permission-based, and designed to feel comfortable for owners who are new to this type of research.

---

### 1. Initial cold outreach

**Subject / first line:**

> "Hi [Name], I'm Lord Wilson from BlackSwanLabz."

**Body:**

> "I run a research operation that finds operational gaps most business owners never see — using only public data.
>
> I've got over 6,000 hours in this work and built a custom intelligence system specifically for spotting these gaps before they become problems. I'd like to be your team member — meaning I do the heavy research, you make the decisions.
>
> If we find something worth acting on, we talk about what that looks like. No pressure, no pitch — just a conversation.
>
> Would you like me to send you a research report on your company and a competitor snapshot?"

**Channel rules:**

- Email = preferred for cold outreach.
- LinkedIn/DM = acceptable, shorter version: same opening, link to a hosted PDF instead of attachment.
- SMS/phone = only if you already have a warm referral or prior contact.

---

### 2. If they reply with interest

**Your response:**

> "Great — I'll send it now.
>
> It's written so you can read the one-page summary and stop there if you want. There's no action required until we talk. I'll follow up in a few days if you have questions.
>
> Would you prefer it as a Gmail attachment, a Google Drive link, or a Dropbox link?"

**After they choose a channel:**

> "Got it — I'll send it to [channel] now.
>
> It's titled [Report Name]. Open it and jump to the one-page summary first — that gives you the whole picture in about 60 seconds."

---

### 3. If they ask how you got their information

> "Everything is pulled from public sources — job postings, filings, press, reviews. No internal data, no NDA required."

---

### 4. If they ask why this is free

> "This is the first-pass research I do before any engagement. You get the map either way. If the gaps I've identified are real, the follow-on work is worth discussing. If not, you still have a clearer picture of your market."

---

### 5. If they ask about cost

> "The research report is free. If we find a repetitive workflow that's costing you time, I can automate it for a fixed price — mapped, built, deployed, tested, and documented. After that, I can maintain it for a small monthly fee so you don't have to manage another piece of technology.
>
> But the research stands on its own. You're getting the map either way."

---

### 6. If they ask "What exactly do you automate?"

> "It depends on what I find in your business.
>
> I'm looking for repetitive work that someone currently has to do manually — moving information, checking things, copying data, generating reports, sending routine communications, updating systems, or doing the same process over and over.
>
> The goal isn't to replace people. It's to remove the boring repetitive work so the people you already pay can spend more time on work that actually requires judgment.
>
> Before I automate anything, I map the existing process, estimate the time involved, identify what can be automated, and define how we'll measure whether it actually saved time.
>
> If you want, I can do the research first and see what's there. If nothing's worth automating, you still have the report. If something is, we talk about the build."

---

### 7. If they prefer a call instead of reading a report

> "If you'd rather I walk through it on a 10-minute call instead of reading a report, just say the word — happy to do that too. We can do it whenever works for you — no calendar link needed."

---

### 8. Delivery confirmation

**Send this with the report:**

> "Here is the report, plain and simple.
>
> How to read it:
> 1. Start with the one-page summary.
> 2. Skim the competitor snapshot.
> 3. If something stands out, we talk.
>
> No action required until we speak. I'll follow up in a few days."

---

### 9. Follow-up cadence

**Day 5 — if no reply:**

> "Just checking in — did the report make sense? No rush, just didn't want it to get lost."

**Day 10 — if still no reply:**

> "I'll leave you to it. I've got this saved for the quarter if you ever want to revisit it. No pressure at all."

**After they engage positively:**

> "Great — what stood out to you? Was there anything in the competitor section that surprised you?"

---

### 10. Closing lines (when the conversation naturally ends)

> "I'm your team member on this — not a vendor. If anything in the report sparks a question later, just reply. I'll be here."
