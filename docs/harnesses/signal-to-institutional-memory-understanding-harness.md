# Signal-to-Institutional-Memory Understanding Harness

## 0. Purpose

A general-purpose understanding and control harness for complex organizational situations.

It does not assume an organization is dysfunctional, that a complaint is true, or that a proposed solution is better than the current system.

It converts raw observations, complaints, claims, process failures, and proposed improvements into verified understanding, corrective action, and institutional memory.

Applicable to HR, sales, operations, dispatch, production, customer service, safety, onboarding, communication, management, AI-assisted workflows, and organizational research.

**This is an understanding and control harness, not primarily an automation harness.**

---

## 1. Core Principle

### Signal ≠ Claim ≠ Fact ≠ Cause ≠ Solution

A complaint is a signal.

A person's explanation is a claim.

Evidence determines what can be established.

Root-cause analysis determines what produced the condition.

A corrective action is an intervention.

Verification determines whether the intervention worked.

Institutional memory preserves validated organizational knowledge.

Therefore:

**Signal → Claim → Evidence → Finding → Cause → Action → Verification → Memory**

Never collapse these stages.

## 3. Signal Rule

**Every complaint is a signal.**

Do not automatically believe it, reject it, blame the target, blame the complainant, automate a response, or classify the organization as dysfunctional.

Instead ask:

1. What exactly was reported?
2. Who reported it?
3. When?
4. Where?
5. What process does it concern?
6. What evidence exists?
7. What evidence is missing?
8. Can the claim be independently verified?
9. What alternative explanations exist?
10. What happened downstream?
11. Was the issue previously reported?
12. Was corrective action attempted?
13. Did corrective action work?
14. Did the problem recur?

---

## 4. Complaint Interpretation Matrix

A signal may resolve to:

- VERIFIED TRUE
- PARTIALLY TRUE
- MISINTERPRETED
- UNSUBSTANTIATED
- INCORRECT CAUSE
- MALICIOUS / BAD-FAITH
- UNRESOLVED

**Unsubstantiated does not necessarily mean false.** It may mean evidence is insufficient.

Likewise, an accurate complaint does not mean the complainant correctly identified the root cause.

---

## 5. Evidence Firewall

Separate:

- **Observation:** directly observed or measured.
- **Report:** something someone says happened.
- **Claim:** proposition extracted from the report.
- **Evidence:** information capable of supporting or contradicting the claim.
- **Finding:** current evidence-supported conclusion.
- **Cause:** mechanism responsible for the observed outcome.
- **Action:** intervention intended to change the system.
- **Outcome:** what actually happened after intervention.
- **Institutional Memory:** validated organizational knowledge retained for future use.

Never convert a person's report directly into an organizational fact.

---

## 6. Context Extraction

For every signal, extract:

- WHO
- WHAT
- WHEN
- WHERE
- WHY / ALLEGED WHY
- HOW
- CONDITIONS
- WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE
- WHAT HAPPENED AFTER
- WHO WAS AFFECTED
- PROCESS INVOLVED
- EVIDENCE EXISTS
- EVIDENCE MISSING

Turn vague statements such as "communication is bad" into testable process statements.

## 8. Failure Localization

Possible failure classes:

- **Detection failure:** problem was not noticed.
- **Communication failure:** information did not reach the right actor.
- **Interpretation failure:** information was misunderstood.
- **Authority failure:** required decision could not be made.
- **Execution failure:** known action was not performed.
- **Verification failure:** nobody checked whether it worked.
- **Recovery failure:** no effective fallback existed.
- **Memory failure:** organizational learning was not preserved.
- **Recurrence failure:** the same problem returned without triggering learning.

Do not stop at "something went wrong." Locate the failed control.

---

## 9. Current-State Assessment

Never assume the proposed system is better.

Classify each capability:

- EXISTS
- PARTIAL
- UNKNOWN
- MISSING
- REDUNDANT
- BETTER THAN PROPOSED
- WORSE THAN PROPOSED
- NOT APPLICABLE

Compare current and proposed systems using evidence.

The objective is not automatically replacement. The objective is to determine whether an additional layer produces measurable improvement.

## 17. Question Engineering Layer

Before investigating anything, construct a good question.

Every question should identify:

- REFERENT
- PROPOSITION
- SCOPE
- AUTHORITY
- OBLIGATION
- TIME
- EVIDENCE
- CONSUMPTION POLICY

Weak:

> Why is communication bad?

Strong:

> For customer-order changes occurring after initial quotation, where does information transfer from sales to operations fail, during what period, according to which records, and what measurable downstream impact results?

A strong question creates an investigation.

```

 ↓

 ↓

 ↓
ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS
 ↓
```

Do not ask only what supports a hypothesis.

Preserve competing explanations rather than allowing majority opinion to become truth.

## 23. Red-Team Questions

Before accepting a conclusion:

1. What if the complaint is wrong?
2. What if the complaint is right but the explanation is wrong?
3. What if the problem is caused upstream?
4. What evidence contradicts our conclusion?
5. What information are we missing?
6. Are we confusing correlation with causation?
7. Are we blaming an individual for a system failure?
8. Does the proposed solution address the mechanism?
9. Does the organization already have a better solution?
10. How would we know if the intervention failed?
11. What happens when AI is wrong?
12. What happens when institutional memory becomes stale?
13. Who is authorized to correct the record?
14. What happens when two sources disagree?
15. Can the organization recover without the AI?

---

## 24. Final Decision States

Every investigation must terminate in an explicit state:

- VERIFIED
- PARTIALLY VERIFIED
- UNVERIFIED
- UNRESOLVED
- INCORRECT
- MISINTERPRETED
- MALICIOUS / BAD-FAITH
- ACTION REQUIRED
- ACTION COMPLETED
- ACTION FAILED
- MONITORING
- NO ACTION REQUIRED

Do not force PASS / FAIL when the evidence does not support it.

## 25. Universal Execution Sequence

For any organizational question:

1. DEFINE THE QUESTION
2. CAPTURE THE SIGNAL
3. IDENTIFY THE CLAIM
4. PRESERVE THE SOURCE
5. EXTRACT CONTEXT
6. MAP THE PROCESS
7. IDENTIFY STAKEHOLDERS
8. SEPARATE CLAIM FROM FACT
9. GATHER EVIDENCE
10. GENERATE COMPETING EXPLANATIONS
11. TEST THE EXPLANATIONS
12. LOCALIZE THE FAILURE
13. IDENTIFY ROOT / CONTRIBUTING CAUSES
14. CHECK EXISTING CONTROLS
15. DESIGN THE SMALLEST SAFE INTERVENTION
16. RUN THE INTERVENTION
17. MEASURE THE RESULT
18. VERIFY THE RESULT
19. CHECK FOR RECURRENCE
20. RECORD VALIDATED KNOWLEDGE
21. UPDATE INSTITUTIONAL MEMORY
22. MONITOR
23. REPEAT

---

## 26. Harness Invariant

> **Do not optimize what you have not understood, do not believe what you have not verified, do not discard signals you have not investigated, and do not allow validated organizational knowledge to disappear when the person who possesses it leaves.**

---

## 27. Relationship to the Future Meta Harness

This document is the **regular understanding harness**.

A future **Meta Harness** should evaluate the harness itself:

- Is it asking the right questions?
- Is it preserving evidence correctly?
- Is it distinguishing evidence from inference?
- Is it detecting its own reasoning failures?
- Is it missing relevant explanations?
- Are its interventions effective?
- Is institutional memory becoming stale?
- Is the harness itself improving?

The regular harness governs understanding.

The meta harness governs the quality and evolution of the understanding process.
