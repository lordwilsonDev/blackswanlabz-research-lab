# Use BlackSwanLabz With Your Own AI

Part of the [resource directory](README.md). A procedure, not evidence: it describes how an agent should use the lab's documented methods.

**BlackSwanLabz is both a research record and an agent-readable operating framework.**

You do not need to understand the entire laboratory before beginning. You do not need to recreate its architecture, install every referenced system, or become an expert in AI engineering.

Start with an AI assistant capable of reading repository files, such as Claude Code, Codex, or another compatible agent.

**Repository:** [https://github.com/lordwilsonDev/blackswanlabz-research-lab](https://github.com/lordwilsonDev/blackswanlabz-research-lab)

## Quick Start

1. Give your AI agent access to this repository.
2. Ask it to read `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`, and `llms.txt`.
3. Tell it to begin the 50-Question OS Bootstrap.
4. Answer the questions one at a time.
5. Allow the agent to identify relevant existing skills, harnesses, SOPs, operational requirements, metrics, and unresolved questions.
6. Review and approve the proposed operating framework before any consequential action.
7. Continue through execution, verification, measurement, and improvement.

**The user supplies intent and judgment. The framework supplies a structured method for turning that intent into operational capability.**

## The Closed-Loop Operating Method

BlackSwanLabz follows a human-governed, evidence-driven control loop:

**INTENT → QUESTIONS → STATE → DESIGN → APPROVAL → EXECUTION → VERIFICATION → MEASUREMENT → FEEDBACK → REFINEMENT → NEXT QUESTION**

Each stage has a specific responsibility.

| Stage         | Purpose                                                                   |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Intent        | Establish what the user wants and why                                     |
| Questions     | Recover missing context without inventing answers                         |
| State         | Distinguish confirmed, inferred, unknown, and conflicting information     |
| Design        | Identify processes, roles, skills, harnesses, SOPs, metrics, and controls |
| Approval      | Confirm scope, authority, and human decisions                             |
| Execution     | Carry out authorized, bounded tasks                                       |
| Verification  | Check results against evidence and acceptance criteria                    |
| Measurement   | Compare observed performance against objectives and baselines             |
| Feedback      | Identify errors, gaps, employee friction, and changed conditions          |
| Refinement    | Revise procedures, controls, and capabilities where justified             |
| Next question | Continue from the highest-value unresolved question                       |

The process is recursive. It does not require a perfect master plan before useful work can begin.

## How Questions Become an Operating System

The initial 50 questions establish the user's operating context. Their answers are **inputs to a compilation process, not the final product**.

The agent should produce, where justified:

- **Organizational state:** objectives, constraints, people, existing systems, evidence, and uncertainty.
- **Operational procedures:** role definitions, workflows, handoffs, exception handling, and SOPs.
- **Skills and harnesses:** reusable procedures, verification checks, and governed execution paths.
- **KPIs:** measurements of actual performance, quality, workload, safety, and operational health.
- **OKRs:** human-approved objectives and measurable results that reflect the organization's priorities.
- **Control mechanisms:** ownership, approval boundaries, escalation, monitoring, and corrective action.
- **Next actions:** the smallest justified step toward the desired state.

Do not manufacture KPIs or OKRs merely to fill a template. Establish the objective, relevant baseline, measurement source, owner, and decision rule. Where evidence is insufficient, mark the proposed metric as provisional.

## AIL and MoIE: Challenge the Operating Assumptions

Before recommending a new process, automation, or organizational change, examine the assumptions behind it.

Use Axiom Inversion Logic to ask:

1. What are we assuming must be true?
2. What if that assumption is false?
3. What alternative explanation or operating method follows?
4. What observable evidence would distinguish the alternatives?
5. What would cause the proposed approach to fail?

Use independent reasoning perspectives where helpful, including operational, human, technical, financial, safety, linguistic, and adversarial perspectives.

**An inversion generates a candidate. Evidence determines whether the candidate is supported.**

## Linguistic and Communication Controls

Natural language is the entry point, but it is not automatically a complete operational specification.

The agent must identify ambiguous referents, undefined terms, implied obligations, unclear ownership, hidden constraints, conflicting instructions, and uncertain timeframes.

Use the lowest-burden question that resolves the uncertainty. Confirm important interpretations with the user rather than silently converting assumptions into policy.

For consequential decisions, record:

**Who requested it → What was understood → What was authorized → What was done → What was observed → What was communicated back.**

This preserves the communication chain between human intent and operational execution.

## Cybernetic Feedback and Continuous Improvement

For each approved operational objective, establish:

- **Desired state:** What should be happening?
- **Observed state:** What is actually happening?
- **Difference:** What is preventing the desired result?
- **Control action:** What intervention is justified?
- **Feedback:** Did the intervention improve the outcome?
- **Learning:** What should be preserved, corrected, or reconsidered?

A recurring failure should trigger investigation into whether the underlying procedure, tool, skill, contract, training, or organizational design needs improvement.

Do not automatically attribute process failures to individual employees.

People-centered conditions, including workload, rest, safety, autonomy, and growth, must remain part of the operating model.

## Reuse Before Rebuilding

Before generating a new skill, harness, SOP, dashboard, or automation:

1. Inspect what already exists in the accessible environment.
2. Determine whether an existing capability can satisfy the requirement.
3. Identify the actual limitation or missing control.
4. Prefer the smallest safe adaptation.
5. Create a new capability only when the evidence justifies it.
6. Preserve the reason for the change and its verification results.

Do not equate a design document with an executable system. Do not claim an unavailable local component has been installed or tested.

## Human Authority and Evidence

The agent may propose, analyze, draft, and test within its authorized environment.

It must not assume permission to change organizational policies, access private employee records, modify production systems, make employment decisions, spend money, or deploy software without appropriate authorization.

Follow `AGENTS.md`, the repository's governance rules, and `CLAIMS.md` when characterizing evidence.

Keep proposed, implemented, tested, verified, pending, and retracted claims distinct.

## Completion Criteria

An onboarding cycle is complete when the user has received:

1. A recorded and reviewable initial state.
2. An inventory of relevant existing and missing capabilities.
3. Proposed operational procedures and ownership.
4. Proposed KPIs and OKRs, with measurement assumptions clearly stated.
5. A list of risks, unresolved questions, and approval requirements.
6. A prioritized next action with acceptance criteria.
7. A clear distinction between recommendations and actions actually performed.

The system should then continue through authorized work and feedback, not restart onboarding unnecessarily.

**Core principle: The user answers questions. The operating framework determines what is needed next. Human judgment retains authority. Evidence determines what has actually been achieved.**
